DISQUALIFYING FAILURES

- 04f-D14 [criteria 3, 4, 5; residual of 04f-D4(b)] 04f:163-169 (headline 5), 1236-1243, 1565-1567, `tbl-sparse-validation` -- The SCA bias in the complete design is asserted as a fact the chapter's own table does not support. Headline 5: "The SCA variance is also biased upward in the complete design." Section 5: "The bias exists in the complete design, where there is no sparsity, so it is not an effect of sparsity." Four lines earlier the same paragraph reports t = 2.16 (p = 0.039, 29 df) at h2 = 0.2 and t = 1.02 (p = 0.32; +21.3 %, s.e. 20.9 %) at h2 = 0.4 and states that neither passes the Bonferroni threshold over the 12 tests (0.0042). By the chapter's own rule one of the two estimates is inside Monte Carlo error and the other is not resolved after correction; "exists" is contradicted by the sentence before it (criterion 5) and is a measurement claim the data do not carry (criteria 3, 4). The follow-on, "the larger values at small k say that sparsity adds to it" (1567), compares medians over heterogeneous scenarios (k = 2: +30.1 %, k = 3: +23.9 %, k = 4: +11.9 %, k = 5: +16.3 %, k = 10: +12.9 %, recomputed) with no dispersion and no pairing to the complete design: an interpretation presented as a result. The "bounded below at zero" explanation is correctly marked untested; the conclusion "not an effect of sparsity" is not.

- 04f-D15 [criteria 3, 4, 5, 6] 04f:1810-1811, 1788-1790, 693 against 1019 and 1747, 1577-1589 -- Statements that the rewritten results no longer support, and one that contradicts the chapter's own text. The author listed several as "noticed, not touched"; they depend on headlines 1, 3, 5 and limitation 7, which changed. (a) 04f:1810-1811 "The evidence is sufficient to adopt sparse designs at stage 1 with confidence" follows, two paragraphs later, "Almost all the evidence ... comes from resampling complete data or from simulation, not from trials designed sparse from the start" (1804-1806), and sits against headline 1 (margin 0.5 s.e., one cell, cheapest of 12), headline 3 (nothing survives Bonferroni), headline 6 (four replicates, nothing survives) and limitation 7 (one dense factorial, not measured elsewhere). The chapter's closing conclusion is stronger than any result it reports. (b) 04f:1788-1790 "This chapter's simulation shows the variance component is not recoverable at stage 1": the simulation shows an upward median bias of +12 to +30 % at k = 2 to 4 and +220 % at k = 1 (with 43 % convergence); "not recoverable" is neither measured (no coverage, no precision, no stage-1 vs product-development comparison) nor the wording of section 5 ("poorly recovered"). (c) 04f:693 "Modelling G x E explicitly helped in every study that tested it [@lubanga2025; @li2026]" contradicts 04f:1019-1021 (G x E term degraded prediction under high sparsity in @hildaono2026, which the chapter cites as having tested it), 04f:1747 ("Fit a G x E term under extreme sparsity" listed under what not to do) and the Hild Aono full text (the G x E models were comparable at 66 % and worse at higher sparsity). (d) The REML box (1577-1589) states a mechanism as established ("The likelihood is flat along that ridge. The algorithm walks to the boundary of the parameter space"; "The variance estimates are not usable, and should not be reported"). Nothing in the chapter or the cached files examines the likelihood surface; and at k = 1 the chapter's own table gives a female-GCA variance median bias of -2.0 %, which does not look "not usable" (the median pools converged and non-converged fits and no split is shown). Interpretation presented as a result and a recommendation resting on it (criterion 4); the limit "convergence at k = 1 is not separated from bias" is not carried (criterion 6).
