# 04g-joint-allocation -- verdict, iteration 1

VERDICT: REJECTED

DISQUALIFYING FAILURES

- 04g-D1 [criterion 1] book/04g-joint-allocation.qmd:417-435, "Two component counts" (and the roxygen of R/18_joint_allocation.R:128-140) -- The text says `n_comp_cross` "is what links GCA contrasts by data" and presents the pair (`n_comp_cross`, `n_comp_loc`) as "the female x male x location metric @sec-sparse-limits asked for". Both statements fail. (a) The two counts are not two conditions. If every location receives a plot, `n_comp_cross = 1` implies `n_comp_loc = 1`: with no shared parent between location sets L1 and L2, every cross has both parents in one set, so the cross graph is disconnected. The second count adds information only when the first already fails. (b) The pair does not detect the three-way confounding that a female x male x location metric exists to detect. Counter-example, run against the installed package: 2 x 2 pools, 2 locations, k = 1, crosses (1,1),(1,2) at location 1 and (2,1),(2,2) at location 2. `joint_connectivity()` returns `n_comp_cross = 1` and `n_comp_loc = 1`, yet `model.matrix(~ loc + f + m)` has rank 3 of 4: the female GCA contrast is aliased with the fixed location contrast, so GCA contrasts are NOT linked by data. The text concedes "not sufficient", but the two reasons it gives (the relationship matrix; shared parents linking only through GCA) do not cover this case. The first reason argues against necessity, not sufficiency. The claim that this pair is the requested three-way metric is therefore not established.

- 04g-D2 [criterion 5] :71-74 (result 3, headline) against :777-779 and :1016-1021 -- The headline says the exchange search "bought no detected accuracy". The chapter defines *detected* as "past two standard errors of the difference" (:777-778). Within the tester family it then reports optimised - start = +0.019 +/- 0.007, z = 2.71, with 4 of 4 cells positive (recomputed from joint_contrasts.csv). By the chapter's own bar that is detected, though not resolved. The headline contradicts the body. Result 2's headline is scoped to the pooled contrast; result 3's is unconditional.

- 04g-D3 [criteria 1, 4] :824-827 -- "With high SCA the steps are larger ... because a cross that is not made has an SCA no replication of other crosses can reveal." This contradicts the model the chapter declares at :150, s ~ N(0, (A_f (x) A_m) sigma2_s). Under that kernel an unmade cross's SCA covaries with that of made relatives: for a shared female and a half-sib male the covariance is 0.25, the off-diagonal the chapter quotes at :216-217. Other crosses therefore partly reveal it. The causal "because" also sits inside a results paragraph, and nothing in the paragraph tests it (acc_unmade by SCA level is in @tbl-joint-made but is not used to support the mechanism).

- 04g-D4 [criteria 4, 5] :121-122 and :1158-1160 -- "More crosses put more lines into data" (:121) and, in "Where this connects", "Distinct crosses keep more lines in the data, the diversity argument of @sec-mating-connects" are offered as the mechanism behind, and the consequence of, result 1. In the circulant family that carries result 1 (:820-835), every line has exactly c >= 2 crosses at c = 8, 4 and 2 (design_circulant, R/16_mating_design.R:80-82). The number of lines in the data is therefore identical across the three budget splits, as the chapter itself states at :117-118 ("Here every line stays in the data"). The measured contrast cannot support a lines-in-data explanation, and the text contradicts its own :117.

- 04g-D5 [criterion 7] :926-927 -- "an earlier, unpaired sweep had suggested the opposite". This cites a result with no source. Nothing in book/data, regenerate.R or any cited reference produces it (consumed from audit 04g-R8).

- 04g-D6 [criterion 7] :317 (`@gonzalezbarrios2019`) -- "reported a 40 % gain in response for the same resources". The source reports a potential increase in selection response of *up to 43 %* for the moderate MED strategy. The figure is wrong, and the ceiling is presented as a realised gain (refs 04g-B1).

- 04g-D7 [criterion 7] :307-309 and :942-943 (`@montesinoslopez2024`) -- The chapter attributes to this paper only lower variability: "a worst-case property a mean accuracy does not show", which implies no mean gain. The source reports superior performance *and* less variability compared with random allocation. The omission then shapes the "mixed record" verdict at :942 (refs 04g-B2).

- 04g-D8 [criterion 7] :323-325 (`@kadam2021`) -- "optimising PEV or CDmean over *all possible* single crosses ... gives a better training set". The source's advantage is in *expected* prediction accuracy, a deterministic forecast, and the authors phrase the conclusion conditionally. The chapter states a realised improvement the source does not claim (refs 04g-B4).

NON-DISQUALIFYING OBSERVATIONS

- 04g-M1 :919-923 -- Tester female coverage "from 0.494 to 0.549, which is the ceiling". Two lines later the text prints the ceiling as 0.55. The mean over replicates is 0.54875, so it is near the ceiling, not at it (audit 04g-R10).
- 04g-M2 :411-412 (and R/18_joint_allocation.R:28-30, :101-102) -- The mean PEV of the GCA effects is called "the A-optimality criterion of @butler2014". In the model-based design literature of that group, the A-value is commonly the mean PEV of *pairwise differences*. That equals the mean PEV of effects only up to the covariance term, and the two need not rank designs identically. I could not confirm the equivalence the text asserts.
- 04g-M3 :390-391 -- "the zero-mean GCA x location terms do not represent one [a subgroup x location interaction]". Under (A_f (x) I_L) sigma2_fl (:153), family members' GCA x location deviations are correlated, so the model does carry a *random* family x location interaction. What it omits is a systematic one. The parenthesis is imprecise on exactly the point the bias argument turns on.
- 04g-M4 :309-310 (`@lell2021`) -- "found equal subgroups in overlapping subsets of environments best". The abstract calls it "one promising design". The superlative is unsupported, and I could not confirm it (refs 04g-B3).
- 04g-M5 :87, :1128-1129, :1031 -- Three claims cannot be checked from any versioned file: "about 30 minutes of CPU, two thirds of it in the exchange searches", and the attribution of three outlier timings to "a machine sleep" (audit 04g-R1, 04g-R6).
- 04g-M6 :832-835, :836-838, :1040-1043, :905-924 -- Several labels and counts are hand-typed next to inline computations: which steps fall under 2 SE, "76 crosses", "152 against 160 plots", "two of the 120 random sparse plans", "24 cells". All are correct now, but they will not follow a regeneration (audit 04g-R3, R4, R5, R7).
- 04g-M7 :940-941 -- "This replicates ... the finding of @sec-sparse that structured layouts matter little". A pooled contrast that is not detected (z = 1.74), plus one family past 2 SE (random, z = 2.29), is presented as a replication. This is interpretation, worded as a result.
- 04g-M8 :1093-1094 -- Checklist item 7 ("Reserve exchange optimisation ... for tester designs") rests on four cells with one optimised design each. Its z = 2.71 is conditional on those fixed designs, so it carries no design-to-design variance, and it is below the chapter's resolved bar. The item is labelled provisional, but its evidence is thinner than the checklist form implies.
- 04g-M9 :62-63 -- The headline says "For the crosses that were made the order reverses". The body (:849-852) shows the 80-vs-160 step not reversed at h2 = 0.2 and high SCA (z = -0.32). The headline's hedge "less firmly" understates a partial reversal.
