## Findings

### H

**09-R1 (H) The relaxation is called an upper bound at a given diversity; it is not.** `09:139-141` "Each row is the best continuous plan at that exchange rate, and therefore an upper bound on what any discrete plan of the same diversity can achieve"; `09:117-123` "the relaxation's optimum is a valid upper bound"; caption `09:144` "Discrete strategies must sit below and to the left of it"; legend `09:164` "(upper bound)". Each row maximises the penalised objective `c'u - lambda c'fc` (R/03_de_select.R, `ocs_relaxation`), so a discrete plan at the same diversity can sit above it. Measured at book scale (`chk1.R`): at a 1% budget the best relaxation point with its own alpha <= 0.01 reaches index 1.0023 while a greedy plan (60-point w grid, 1e-6 to 1e-1) at alpha = 0.0069 has index 1.4227; `ocs_bound(ctx, 60, 0.01, gd_ref)` = 1.5532 (certified, `converged` TRUE). This is the construction CLAUDE.md records as invalid and chapter 10 already corrects (`10:818-832`, `10:686`). `09:129-131` hedges one step ("needs one more step ... in @sec-advanced") but the three statements above and the figure are not conditioned. Also "below and to the left" is the wrong direction: a dominated plan lies below or to the RIGHT (more loss) of an increasing curve. Fix: say the relaxation is an upper bound only for the penalised objective (or at lambda = 0, unconstrained cap problem), point to `ocs_bound()`/@sec-bound for the bound on the constrained problem, reword caption and legend ("continuous relaxation, not a ceiling"), "below or to the right".

**09-R2 (H) The convergence demonstration compares the wrong statistic.** `09:170-183`. The callout says an under-converged relaxation "returns a value smaller than the discrete optimum". The chunk prints the INDEX: 50 iterations 1.606, 4000 iterations 1.987, `greedy_w3` 1.992 -- so the CONVERGED relaxation is also below the discrete plan, which the callout names as the symptom of non-convergence. The converged relaxation has lower index because it is more diverse (theta 0.6862 against 0.6876 for the discrete plan) and pays lambda = 10 per unit theta. The valid comparison is the penalised value `V = index - lambda*theta` (`chk1.R`): 50 iters -5.194, 500 -4.879, 1000 -4.8754, 2000 -4.8748, 4000 -4.8748, 16000 -4.87479; discrete truncation plan -4.8840. Only V shows the intended ordering (50 iters below the discrete plan, converged above). Also: "4000 is the minimum that converged in testing" (`09:174`) has no tolerance and no test in the chapter (V changes 8e-6 between 4000 and 16000 at lambda = 10, 2.7e-4 at lambda = 100); `ocs_bound()` now doubles until the value stops moving (tol 1e-3) and carries `converged`; cite it instead of a fixed count. Printed labels `index_50_iters.index` etc. (names doubled by `c()` of named scalars) -- use `unname()`.

**09-R3 (H) The "greedy frontier" is one point and the "knee" does not exist.** `09:202-223`, caption `09:205` "The knee is where further diversity costs index steeply", and the `greedy_w1`, `greedy_w3` points of the first figure (`09:151-152`). `sel_greedy` scores `w*index - theta`; theta increments are O(10^-3), index differences O(1), so every `w >= ~0.03` returns the truncation plan. Measured: of the 11 grid values `ws` (`09:207`), 10 give the identical row (alpha 0.0412, index 1.9919, Ne_par 13.02; only `w = 0` differs: alpha -0.0135, index 0.1343), and `greedy_w1`, `greedy_w3` coincide with `truncation` in the first figure (three labels on one point). The window where the sweep moves is w in [1e-5, 1e-2] (alpha -1.35% to 3.95%, index 0.21 to 1.99; chapter 10 states exactly this, `10:316-335`, "a sweep in the wrong units, not a flat frontier"; chapter 07 uses w = 3e-4). The figure has two distinct points joined by a line; "choose its knee" is not demonstrated. Fix: grid `10^seq(-5, -1.5, length = ~14)` (and `greedy_w1/w3` -> w values inside the window), or cross-reference 10's sweep; add `stopifnot(!anyDuplicated(round(front[, c("alpha","index")], 8)))` so a degenerate sweep breaks the render.

**09-R4 (H) The penalty is not "in units the budget defines".** `09:40-42` "a plan that overshoots by 10% of the budget is penalised in units the budget defines, whatever the absolute value of alpha_max". `make_fitness` (printed at `09:37`): `viol <- max(0, (gd_ref - gd)/gd_ref - alpha_max)`, i.e. the excess in ABSOLUTE alpha units, times `penalty = 1000`. A 10% overshoot of a 1% budget costs 1000 x 0.001 = 1.0; of a 5% budget 1000 x 0.005 = 5.0 (computed). The penalty is linear in the violation, not relative to the budget. Same wording in the roxygen of `make_fitness` ("proportional"), `R/03_de_select.R:165`. Fix the sentence (linear in the excess alpha, same price per unit of alpha at every budget), or divide by `alpha_max` in the code (that changes every DE number in the book: CLAUDE.md `max_use`/bit-for-bit rule applies; the prose fix is the cheap one).
