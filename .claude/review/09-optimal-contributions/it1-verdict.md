## DISQUALIFYING FAILURES

- **09-D1 [criterion 1, 6] 09:115-119, 139-141, 144, 164 (relaxation as "upper bound").** The chapter says the relaxation is a valid upper bound, that each row bounds "any discrete plan of the same diversity", and that discrete strategies "must sit below and to the left of it". The rows maximise the penalised objective c'u - lambda c'fc, so this is false for the constrained problem. Recomputed: at the 1% budget the best relaxation point with alpha <= 0.01 has index 1.0023; a greedy plan at alpha = 0.00685 (60 values of w, 1e-6 to 1e-1) has index 1.4227, above it; `ocs_bound(ctx, 60, 0.01, gd_ref)` = 1.5532 (lambda* = 68.1, 8000 iterations, converged TRUE). The 0.01 curve point therefore sits 0.42 BELOW a feasible discrete plan. "Below and to the left" is also the wrong direction for a dominated plan on an increasing curve. The one hedge (09:129-131) does not condition the three statements or the figure. Contradicts chapter 10 (10:683-686, 818-832), 10b:744-752 and CLAUDE.md, which already name this construction as invalid. Absorbs R1 and C1.
- **09-D2 [criterion 1, 4] 09:169-183 (convergence callout).** The callout says under-convergence returns a value "smaller than the discrete optimum". The chunk prints the index: 50 iters 1.606, 4000 iters 1.987, greedy_w3 1.992. The converged relaxation is also below the discrete plan (recomputed: 1.9873 vs 1.9919), so the printed numbers contradict the symptom named. The valid statistic is V = index - lambda*theta: -5.1939 (50), -4.8790 (500), -4.8748 (4000), -4.874792 (16000). "4000 is the minimum that converged in testing" has no tolerance and no test in the chapter, and `ocs_bound()` already replaces it with doubling to a tolerance. Absorbs R2 and C22.
- **09-D3 [criterion 3, 4] 09:202-223 (greedy frontier, "the knee").** The frontier is degenerate. Recomputed: of the 11 chapter values of w, 10 return the identical plan (alpha 0.04122, index 1.9919, equal to truncation); only w = 0 differs (alpha -0.0135, index 0.1343). The figure is two points joined by a line; "choose its knee" and the caption "the knee is where further diversity costs index steeply" are not demonstrated by it. The same collapse puts `greedy_w1`, `greedy_w3` and `truncation` on one point in the first figure. The sweep moves only for w roughly in [1e-5, 1e-2] (39 distinct points among my 60-value grid). Chapter 10 (10:316-335) says this. The chapter presents a null result as a frontier and says nothing about it. Absorbs R3.
- **09-D4 [criterion 1] 09:40-42 (penalty "in units the budget defines").** `make_fitness` (printed at 09:37): `viol <- max(0, (gd_ref - gd)/gd_ref - alpha_max)`, times `penalty = 1000`. The violation is the absolute excess in alpha units. Recomputed: a 10% overshoot costs 1.0 at a 1% budget and 5.0 at a 5% budget. It is linear in the excess and not relative to the budget. The sentence describes a different function. Absorbs R4 and C21 (penalty part).
- **09-D5 [criterion 5] 09:187-189 vs 06:884-896 ("maximum attainable alpha"); 09:22, 225-262, 341 ($\alpha_{\max}$ used for two quantities); 09:230, 255, 290 ($\bar H_{\text{ref}}$); 09:57, 71, 85-90, 324 ($\mathbf G$ for three matrices).** Same chapter and book, symbol and definition drift: (i) truncation "defines the maximum attainable alpha" is false as stated (a minimum-diversity subset exceeds it; 06 says so and calls it the alpha at which the budget stops binding); (ii) `alpha_max` is the user's budget at 09:22, 26, 185, 209, 341 and the truncation ceiling at 09:244, 255-258, 288; (iii) the reference gene diversity is $GD_{\text{ref}}$ in 06 and 90, but $\bar H_{\text{ref}}$ here, and $\bar H$ is the heterosis mean elsewhere; (iv) $\mathbf G$ is genetic covariance (09:57, 71), Gomez-Romano's coancestry (09:85-90, 324) and, in 90, the VanRaden GRM. $\alpha$ is never defined in the chapter. Absorbs C2, C3, C4, C5, R11, R12.
