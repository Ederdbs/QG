## THE CONTESTED POINT: who is right

The writer is right; my it1 wording ("a discrete plan at the same diversity can beat a relaxation point", and the same phrase in CLAUDE.md, in the `ocs_bound()` roxygen and in 10b) was imprecise.

Derivation. Let c_S be the equal-weight vector of any set S with |S| = n. It is feasible in @eq-ocs-V (sum 1, 0 <= c_i <= 1/n). Hence V(lambda) >= c_S'u - lambda c_S'f c_S = ubar_S - lambda theta_S. With V(lambda) = ubar_lam - lambda theta_lam this gives ubar_S <= ubar_lam + lambda (theta_S - theta_lam). At theta_S <= theta_lam the right side is <= ubar_lam, for every lambda >= 0 (the sign of lambda is not even needed at equality). So a relaxation point bounds every discrete plan at least as diverse as itself; at exactly equal diversity a plan cannot beat it. The construction "best relaxation point whose own alpha is inside the budget" fails for a different reason: a plan that spends the budget has theta_S > theta_lam of a point strictly inside it, the inequality is then the tangent of slope lambda at the point, and the tangent lies above the point. The chapter states exactly this (statement 2 and 3 after @eq-ocs-V, @eq-ocs-weak) and gives the correct bound (@eq-ocs-bound). The it1 D1 failure stands as it was: the it1 chapter claimed the point's height was a ceiling for plans at the budget, and that is false (numbers below). What is wrong is only my phrase "same diversity", which the author is right to refuse.

Consequence outside 09: `R/12_search.R` `ocs_bound()` roxygen is corrected (checked in the diff); CLAUDE.md and 10b:641-643 still carry "at that diversity ... A discrete plan can beat it, and does". See 09-M12.
