VERDICT: REJECTED

## DISQUALIFYING FAILURES

- 06-D1 [criterion 1, 5] qmd:190-195 -- "GD and N_s answer 'how much heterozygosity does this selection preserve', the right question for next season's hybrid vigour." GD = 1 - theta_S is the EXPECTED heterozygosity of the merged set (H_e, @eq-catalogue-gd-he). Hybrid vigour rides on the OBSERVED heterozygosity of the F1s, H_o = GD_T + GD_BS (00:440-447; 05:345-356), which moves the other way under truncation: GD z = -18 while GD_WI_hyb rises 0.1814 -> 0.1823 in this chapter's own @tbl-catalogue-gd-partition (audit; 05 reports realised F1 heterozygosity z = +2.5). Expected-for-observed heterozygosity is an explicit rejection ground, and the chapter's own table contradicts the sentence. Endorses 06-C2 (H). 06:119-120 ("5 % of expected heterozygosity") is fine in itself; the defect is the hybrid-vigour gloss.

- 06-D2 [criterion 5] qmd:12-14 (and title, 06:1) -- "every one operates on the molecular coancestry matrix". Contradicted inside the chapter: theta_from_freq/he_nei read colMeans(X) (06:101-107), alleles_lost is a MAF count (06:231-264), N_e,par and ne_lines_pool are pedigree-only ("a metric independent of f", 06:199; "needs no fL"), theta_pools/gd_partition read fL, F_hom/F_drift in the z table are frequency lenses (00:463-465, 01:513-531). The common signature is the true part. Open verbatim since 01-C7. Endorses 06-C1 (H) and 06-R1 (medium); I rate it disqualifying because it is a false statement of what the catalogue's objects are, not a stylistic overstatement.

- 06-D3 [criterion 7, 4] qmd:260-264 -- "The mechanism connects directly to @sec-genomic-era: heterozygosity-maximising management pushes allele frequencies toward 0.5, which favours rare alleles in the short run but does not stop them drifting to loss once they are no longer the rarest; allele-count management defends every segregating allele directly." Verified against the paper (lc.txt, pp. 2, 7-8). The "intermediate frequencies, rare alleles less likely to be lost" mechanism is stated for an UNDIVIDED population (Fernandez et al. 2004; "In undivided populations, maxHT can be the most appropriate method ... for conserving both a high heterozygosity and a high number of alleles"). For SUBDIVIDED populations the paper says only that max-H_T "is made at the cost of an increase of homozygosity ... in each subpopulation, at least in the short term, and a substantial loss of alleles". The step "once they are no longer the rarest" appears nowhere. Moreover the paper's one explanation of why allele-count management loses alleles (p. 2: "some rare alleles were maintained at low frequencies, being more likely to be eventually lost") runs against the book's "allele-count management defends every segregating allele directly". The empirical sentence before it (06:254-259) is faithful; the mechanism is the book's own, placed directly under the paper's name with the article "The mechanism", and presented as result, not conjecture. Endorses 06-B1 (WRONG).

- 06-D4 [criterion 7] qmd:497 -- "@lopezcortegano2019 choose lambda for conservation, where no such derivation applies." The paper (p. 3, verified) runs lambda = 1 mainly but also 0, 1000 and "lambda = 0.5, a value suggested to maximize the total genetic variance of a hypothetical quantitative trait (Bennewitz and Meuwissen, 2006)". A derivation-based value is in the source, and it coincides with the chapter's own lambda_d = 1/2 at flat d. The sentence misstates the source on exactly the point it uses to set the chapter's derivation apart. Endorses 06-B2 (WRONG, nuance); I do not accept it as mere nuance because the chapter uses the sentence to scope the paper. Bennewitz & Meuwissen 2006 not read.

- 06-D5 [criterion 5, 7] qmd:248-251 -- "Among the metrics of the redundancy analysis in @sec-choosing, it [alleles_lost] is the one genuinely non-redundant with theta -- a claim that chapter tests rather than asserts." False against the cited source. I recomputed from book/data/metric_redundancy.csv: Spearman(theta, alleles_lost_MAF) = 0.932, Spearman(theta, Ne_parents) = -0.925, Spearman(theta, eff_dim) = -0.967. 07:199-209 lists MAF-threshold allele loss among |rho| >= 0.93 metrics; the metrics that retain rank s.d. after regressing on theta are allelic richness (alleles RETAINED, 52 %) and N_e,par (36 %). So the chapter names the wrong metric, says "the one" where 07 gives two, and contradicts its own 06:208-209/224-227 ("N_e,par catches what coancestry blurs"). Endorses 06-R12 (medium) and raises it: the sentence cites a result the source does not contain. The second half of R12 (F_drift "more strongly than any other metric", Gst 36.1 vs F_drift 36.0 in the full output) is scoped by the chapter to the table; non-disqualifying (see M list).

- 06-D6 [criterion 3 provenance, 7] qmd:322-323 -- "The package keeps its definition because the cached redundancy analysis of @sec-choosing and the case-study z-scores were computed with it." book/data/metric_redundancy.csv and book/figs/metric_redundancy.png have no producer in R/, inst/scripts/ or book/scripts/ (grep: referenced only by 07, index, 92) and 07 does not label them as an external cache. The sentence asserts a provenance nobody can check, and the data contradict it for the one column checkable: the csv eff_dim spans 1.438-1.506 (mean 1.486); I computed the package eff_dim() on a random 60-subset at book scale = 26.19 and the UNCENTERED participation ratio = 1.47. The cached column therefore looks like the uncentered form that 06:350-357 calls a mistake. Scale differences (12000 markers vs 2000) do not turn 26 into 1.5. A cached external result must be labelled as such and never presented as computed with the repository's definition. Endorses 06-R13 (medium) and raises it; the eff_dim discrepancy is a 07 finding, owed to 07, but the sentence in 06 is false-as-stated or unverifiable either way.

- 06-D7 [criterion 3] qmd:638-649 -- trunc_cap: "z = -1.4 on GD, close to the random subsets ... A constraint on GD alone would not flag it." One founder draw (seed 1), no threshold stated, no dispersion. I reran 20 simulation seeds (null B = 200, B_full = 300, seed 7): trunc_cap GD z ranges -4.16 to -0.35, mean -2.06, |z| > 2 on 12 of 20 draws; ANE z 14.0-18.8 on 20/20. The robust statement is "GD z small relative to ANE z"; "would not flag it" holds on 8 of 20 draws under any conventional threshold. Unit of replication is the founder draw; the claim is a qualitative comparison on one. Endorses 06-R14 (medium) and raises it because the sentence is the chapter's closing recommendation and "this chapter does not report" the Monte Carlo error does not cure it (criterion 3, Monte Carlo error carried by every simulated estimate). Same pattern, smaller: 06:224-225 "the order reverses" holds on 18/20 seeds per the audit.

- 06-D8 [criterion 3, 5] qmd:631-641 and tbl-catalogue-z -- the headline z values for the same example, same seed (null seed 7, 1000 full subsets) differ between chapters: 06 prints -18.0 (GD) and 36.0 (F_drift); 05 (tbl-era-signature-z) prints -17.06 and 35.08. I reproduced both: B = 400 gives GD -18.01, F_hom 9.45, F_drift 35.97; B = 2000 gives -17.06, 9.41, 35.08 (the full draws depend on B because the cheap draws consume the stream first). Neither caption states B, both say "seed 7, 1000 subsets", so a reader sees two numbers for one quantity. The ~1 z-unit gap (about 5 %) is the Monte Carlo error of an s.d. from 1000 draws (relative s.e. ~2.2 %) plus the shifted null mean; the table reports no interval ("Monte Carlo error of the s.d. not shown"), so the chapter cannot tell the reader the two numbers agree. Endorses 06-C4 (M) and upgrades it: criterion 3 requires the Monte Carlo error with every simulated estimate, and criterion 5 forbids a number that changes between chapters. Also gd_ref 0.32584 vs 0.32586 and alpha(truncation) 4.122 % vs 4.129 % (continuity re-run, consistent with my gd_ref values 0.3258386 vs 0.3258629).
