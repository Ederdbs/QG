## 1. Findings

Severity: **D** = a claim the chapter's own data or code do not support; **M** = a number, sentence or code claim to fix; **L** = form or economy.

### Code (R/18) and its tests

- **04g-R1 [M] `R/18_joint_allocation.R:20-21` and the roxygen of `joint_alloc`: "no location may exceed `ceiling(n * k / nLoc)` plots" is false for the coverage rule.** `pick <- order(-gain, load, runif)[seq_len(k)]` takes the k best locations even when fewer than k are below the cap (the capped ones carry gain `-Inf` but are still chosen). Measured over 100 seeds per sweep configuration (`chk2.R`): violations (max load = cap + 1) in circulant c = 2, k = 4 (the chapter's "40 crosses at four locations"): 20 of 100; circulant c = 4, k = 2: 2 of 100; the other four configurations 0. Effect on the sweep is small (a location with one extra plot), but the roxygen is a stated guarantee and `tests/testthat/test-joint.R` tests load balance only for c = 4, k = 1, nLoc = 4 (`diff(range(colSums)) <= 1`), never k > 1. Either fix the rule (reserve capacity) or state "tries to" and test it at k = 4.
- **04g-R2 [M] 04g:1277-1279 (the list of what `test-joint.R` checks): "the coverage rule balances loads and covers more of each pool than random placement".** The test (`test-joint.R:17-24`) checks `fem_cover` only, not `male_cover`, one configuration, k = 1. Loads are balanced to 1 there only; R1 shows they are not at k = 4. Reword to what is tested, or add the male check and the k = 4 case.
- **04g-R3 [OK, verified] `joint_connectivity()` against an independent oracle.** `chk.R`: a literal label-propagation count of (i) the female x male graph of planted crosses and (ii) the location graph joined through shared parents, on 60 random 8 x 8 allocations (k in 1:2, random plans, a location blanked every fifth draw): 0 mismatches with `n_comp_cross` and `n_comp_loc`. The unit test only covers hand-built NC II cases; the oracle is not in the repo. Suggest keeping it as a `ref_*`-style test (CLAUDE.md principle 3). Edge not covered: an `M` with no planted cell gives `max(integer(0))` = `-Inf` with a warning (L).
- **04g-R4 [OK, verified] `joint_pev()`** equals the GCA block of the explicitly inverted coefficient matrix (test-joint.R:30-34 passes in the knit session's package); the rank/alias example (`tbl-joint-alias`) asserts rank 3 of 4 columns and both counts 1.
- **04g-R5 [L] `joint_alloc(strategy = "optimised")` with `start` supplied still runs the full greedy coverage loop first** (R/18:65-76) and discards it; harmless but it consumes RNG draws, so the stream after the call depends on whether `start` was passed. Document or skip the loop.
- **04g-R6 [M] 04g:1021 "Coverage (the `parentage` allocation of @sec-sparse under another name)" and 04g:79-80 (Owed: parentage = coverage, added in the 04f review).** The two rules are not the same allocation. R/17 `parentage` cuts the complete factorial into `nM` cyclic groups and optimises pairwise location concurrence by exchange; R/18 `coverage` is a greedy parent-presence rule on any plan with no concurrence term. Measured on the complete 20 x 20 factorial, 8 locations, k = 2 (`chk.R`, one draw): both give `fem_cover` = `male_cover` = 1 and equal loads, but `all(Mc == Mp)` is FALSE; `conc_hmean` 13.4 (coverage) vs 0 (parentage), `frac_disconn` 0 vs 0.286. They agree on parent coverage and differ on connectivity between locations. "Plays the same role" (04g:79) is defensible; "under another name" is not. One draw, so a direction, not a rate; a one-line check of the claim on a few seeds is the remedy. 04f:1257 uses the same identification ("the rule that @sec-joint calls `coverage`").

### Claims against the chapter's own data

- **04g-R7 [M] 04g:1298-1300 (owed: result 4 now a hypothesis) "the hypothesis of result 4 ... turned into an allocation rule and tested here".** The wording now carries the hypothesis status, but "tested here" is too strong. The sweep contrast `coverage - random` (pooled +0.0057 +- 0.0033, z 1.7, 16 of 24 cells positive) measures the accuracy value of the coverage increment (0.676 -> 0.874 female coverage in factorial plans) at fixed plan, k and loads. It does not separate parent coverage from shared-hybrid concurrence, which is what result 4 of 04f distinguishes (coverage also changes concurrence; it is not controlled). A non-detected effect at z 1.7 is also not evidence against. Say "applied here as an allocation rule; its accuracy gain was not detected and the sweep does not isolate the mechanism".
- **04g-R8 [M] 04g:67-71 and 04g:935-949 "by a margin that grows with the SCA variance".** Cell means (coverage, circulant): 160 -> 40 crosses falls by 0.087 (h2 0.4, low) and 0.147 (high); 0.064 (0.2, low) and 0.096 (high). Point estimates, in the stated direction; no difference of differences with a standard error is computed, and the cells are independent so one is available (`sqrt(se1^2 + se2^2 + ...)`). State it as point estimates or compute it.
- **04g-R9 [M] 04g:950-955 "The two-tester design ... sits below the circulant plan ... in every scenario and strategy".** `tst_below` compares point estimates of 12 cells (3 strategies x 4 scenarios, `acc_all.x < acc_all.y`); no z. The plans also differ in two ways other than the testers (76 vs 80 crosses; 18 lines with two crosses each, so female coverage cannot exceed 0.55, the chapter's own computation at 04g:1038-1044). Report z per cell, or "in the point estimates".
- **04g-R10 [M] 04g:957-975, `tbl-joint-made`: `acc_made` compares correlations over different sets.** In `score()` (`regenerate.R:~815`) `acc_made = cor(blup[made], g_target[made])`: 160, 80 and 40 crosses are different subsets of the 400 hybrids, so the variance of the target values and the restricted-range correlation both change with the set, and the 40-cross set is also the one whose crosses are most correlated through shared parents. "40 crosses at four locations estimate those crosses at 0.845 against 0.807 for 160 at one" compares two different estimands. The chapter says "estimate *those* crosses" but not that the sets differ. One sentence, or an RMSE-type metric that does not depend on the spread of the made set. (Analogue of 04e-D20, estimand undeclared; here the estimand is declared in words but not its non-comparability.)
- **04g-R11 [M] 04g:1113-1118 "within a cluster the strategies differ by 0.3 to 4.7 % in PEV (median 1.8 %)".** `spread` is computed over `random`, `coverage` and `optimised` rows of one configuration. For the circulant plans the plan is deterministic, so the difference is strategy only. For the random-sparse and tester configurations the `optimised` row is the PEV of the single plan `p0` of the optimiser cell (`regenerate.R:~838`), while `random`/`coverage` rows are means over 30 replicate plans: the spread there mixes plan-to-plan variation with strategy. Report the circulant clusters alone, or the `coverage - start` and `optimised - start` differences, which are on one plan. The same applies to the points of `fig-joint-pev` (r = -0.96).
- **04g-R12 [L] 04g:1138-1139 "a millisecond for the greedy start".** Typed, not computed. Measured here: `joint_alloc("coverage")` at 20 x 20, 8 locations, k = 2: 1.25 ms elapsed; a `joint_pev` call 127 ms (so 600 proposals at roughly 0.08 s each is the 47 s). Compute inline or drop.
- **04g-R13 [L] 04g:98-104, 1266-1271 "about 30 minutes of CPU in one unrecorded timing".** The three file-writes are not timed anywhere; the only timings in the repo are the 24 optimiser cells (16.4 min summing the 21 below 600 s, as the chapter prints). The rerun in progress will give a measured number; replace "30 minutes" by it when it finishes.
- **04g-R14 [L] 04g:523-529 (THREAD, owed from 05: `n_unc` prints 2e4 with the x10 dropped).** Still open. The knit renders `` `r n_unc` `` as `2 &times; 10<sup>4</sup>`, which is the markup the `fmt_sci()` helper of `_common.R` was written to avoid for Typst/Pandoc; format it as a count, e.g. `format(n_unc, big.mark = ",")`, for 20,000. The share 0.348 (Monte Carlo s.e. about 0.003 at 20,000) and 0.001 are consistent with a binomial draw of `round(c * 20)` cells.
