## DISQUALIFYING FAILURES

- **04e-D16 [criterion 7] 04e:696-700 -- numbers of Melchinger et al. (2023) attributed to the wrong data set.**
  The chapter writes "@melchinger2023 (their data set DS2, 96 training hybrids) found, for hybrids with both parents untested (H0), the accuracy maximal at c = 1 ... exceeding c = 2 and c = 4 by about 1 to 3 % and 5 to 10 %, with attainable accuracies at c = 1 of 0.50 to 0.65 ... and 0.70 to 0.85". Full text fetched here (Europe PMC XML of PMC10397156): the "~1-3 % and ~5-10 %" sentence belongs to the paragraph on the first data set (Fig. 2, "Using a fixed size N_TS = 96 ... as a benchmark"); the following paragraph opens "The curves of r_a for data set DS2 showed essentially the same picture as those for DS1, with some trends being amplified (Fig. 3)" and says only that for N_TS = 96 "the maximum r_a value for H0 hybrids showed ... a larger difference between c = 1 and c = 4 than for DS1". Only 0.50-0.65 / 0.70-0.85 are DS2 numbers. The chapter's parenthesis covers both. The direction (reported wrongly in iteration 1) is now right, and the DS2 differences are larger than the figure the chapter attaches to DS2. Same class as 04e-D4(b) of iteration 1 (a numeric claim carried by the wrong trait), same criterion.

- **04e-D17 [criteria 3, 5] 04e:917-922, 1340-1342 (with 04e:72-83) -- "the design's advantage is entirely conditional on the estimator", "unconditional", and "testers ... are the only design robust to least squares" are contradicted by the chapter's own least-squares rows.**
  The new LS column of @tbl-mating-formula-sparse and @tbl-mating-ls-sweep give, at 2,000 plots, LS accuracy: tester k=4 0.670; random sparse c=10 0.756 (+0.086, unpaired z = 18.1); NC II 0.737 (+0.067, z = 13.3); circulant c=10 0.707 (+0.036, z = 4.3); random sparse c=5 0.689 (+0.018, z = 3.1); relationship-guided c=5 0.740. The in-repo core sweep agrees for two of them (random c=10 LS 0.760, NC II 0.756, against tester k=4 0.671). So, at equal plots, several sparse or incomplete designs beat every tester under least squares; the penalty for least squares is large only for the sparsest circulant layouts (c = 2, 3, 5), and "the design's advantage is entirely conditional on the estimator" and the prescription "if you cannot fit a mixed model, use testers" (decision step 1, fig-mating-decision node 1) do not follow from the evidence the chapter presents. Intro result 2 ("the analysis matters more than the design") rests on the same generalisation from the sparsest cells. The text sits unchanged since iteration 1, but the claim depends on the table this iteration added and on the LS rows the chapter prints; it is a conclusion the chapter's data contradict, stated as unconditional.

- **04e-D18 [criteria 4, 5] 04e:1282-1286 -- the interpretation of the frontier is not what @tbl-mating-frontier shows.**
  "Once the field is full, the next unit of money belongs in a larger candidate set -- more lines to select among -- not in more plots per cross." Recomputed from `mating_optimizer_frontier.csv`: the optimum is 150 lines at 60,000, and 200 lines (the largest value in the grid, n_test in {50, 100, 150, 200}) at every budget from 80,000 on; from 80,000 to 125,000 the optimum raises reps from 2 to 3 to 4 at c = 3, i.e. the extra money goes to plots per cross, and at 150,000 and 200,000 the field cap (3,000 plots) binds and the optimum is identical (resp 8.086): nothing is bought. A larger candidate set than 200 is not in the grid, so the sentence is neither measured nor derivable from the table, and rows 3-4 run against it. It sits in the paragraph rewritten for 04e-D15 and is presented as the reading of the result.
