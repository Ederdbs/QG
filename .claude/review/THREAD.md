## 01-coancestry -- Coancestry, and the two matrices
- Question: how the hybrid marker matrix $\mathbf X$ is built from the line genotypes, why the VanRaden $\mathbf G$ cannot measure diversity (the total of its elements is zero by construction), and what the molecular coancestry $\mathbf f$ used instead is, including what its scale is and is not, and the two published treatments that arrive at it. It follows 00 because 00 has already defined IBD/IBS, coancestry, $s_i$, $F_i$ and $GD_S = 1 - \theta_S$ in words and points here for @eq-x-freq, @eq-hybrid-dosage and @eq-f-molecular; 01 supplies that computation and the contrast with $\mathbf G$ (01:8-22).
- Establishes:
  - Hybrid dosage $M^H_{(ab)k} = (M^L_{ak} + M^L_{bk})/2 \in \{0,1,2\}$ from homozygous parents, a deterministic genotype (@eq-hybrid-dosage, 01:24-45); F1 heterosis = sum of $d_k$ over the loci where the parents differ, no epistasis, $d_k$ defined at first use as the departure of the heterozygote from the homozygote midpoint (01:41-46, pointing to @eq-het-value); $x_{ik} = M^H_{ik}/2 \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$ and $\mathbf X = \mathbf M^H/2$ (@eq-x-freq, 01:48-61).
  - VanRaden $\mathbf G = \mathbf Z\mathbf Z'/(2\sum_k p_k(1-p_k))$ with $p_k$ in the hybrid population (@eq-vanraden-g, 01:71-80); `vanraden_G()` takes the 0/1/2 matrix $\mathbf M^H$ and returns a list `G, Z, p`, while `molecular_coancestry()` takes 0/0.5/1 $\mathbf X$ (01:81-90).
  - $\mathbf 1'\mathbf G\mathbf 1 = 0$ (@eq-g-sum-zero, 01:92-102), so $\theta_S = (2n^2)^{-1}\mathbf 1'\mathbf G_{SS}\mathbf 1 = 0$ and $N_s = 1/(2\theta_S)$ diverges on $\mathbf G$: `sum_G` $-4.179 \times 10^{-11}$, `Ns_on_G` $-9.349 \times 10^{15}$ (round-off reciprocal, no information), $\min \mathbf G = -0.1389$ (@tbl-g-sums, 01:103-134). $\mathbf G\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 0$, so $\mathbf G$ is singular (01:412-416).
  - On $\mathbf f$ (biallelic markers) $\theta_S \in [1/2, 1]$ and $N_s \in [1/2, 1]$ for every set: whole population 0.7442, 1000 random subsets 0.6943-0.7445 (@tbl-ns-on-f, 01:111-114, 01:137-150); a monotone rescaling, not a head count (@sec-ns).
  - Subset sum under random sampling without replacement: $\mathrm E[\mathbf 1_S'\mathbf G\mathbf 1_S] = n\frac{N-n}{N-1}\overline G_{ii}$, not a function of $n/N$ alone, zero at $n = N$; Monte Carlo 75.7129 against formula 75.5192, s.e. 0.1065 (n = 100, 20000 draws, @tbl-subset-sum-mc), and exactly $n\overline G_{ii} + n(n-1)(\sum G - \operatorname{tr}G)/(N(N-1))$ (01:152-195). A subset sum is a well-defined distance from the population centroid, not a coancestry of the subset (01:165-168).
  - The failure of $\mathbf G$ is that its centring constant $2\mathbf p$ is estimated from the set being measured, so the reference moves with the set (callout, 01:198-208).

## 02-subdivided -- Diversity in a subdivided population

It follows 01 because 01 defines the molecular coancestry $\mathbf f$ between single individuals; 02 averages the same quantity over the individuals of subpopulations, states the partition in those terms, and then makes the step to hybrids partitioned over parent lines (02:5-24, @sec-keystone).

## 05b-heterosis-dominance -- Heterosis, dominance, and what divergence buys
- Question: whether between-pool divergence is "the heterosis engine" that the book had asserted since 02, and what follows once the claim is derived: the expected heterosis of a pool pair, its exact split into a shared term ($GD_T$) and a divergence term ($GD_{BS}$) under stated conditions, why neutral divergence relocates expected heterosis from the one to the other without adding any, what does buy heterosis (divergence aligned with dominance), the combining-ability decomposition of heterosis (GCA/SCA) and how neutral divergence reshapes it, and eight steps to run this on a programme's data. It follows 02b because 00, 01, 02 and 02b each state a piece (the numerical identity, $d_k$ and $H_i$, the partition and the trade-off, the two GCA statements) and hand the derivation or the decomposition to it; it opens by listing those hand-overs (05b:26-41) and claims two halves, *maintain* ($GD_{BS}$ lost at fixed $GD_T$ is heterosis lost, one for one) and *do not maximise* (neutral divergence adds none) (05b:43-54).
- Establishes:
  - Premises (05b:73-113, @sec-heterosis-premises): inbred parents; dominance within loci and additivity between (no epistasis, which $d_k$ cannot absorb); the marker panel is the set of dominance loci. The chapter's $d_k$ is one gamma(2, 4) draw (seed 4), positive at every locus, an illustrative assumption, not varied: every number that depends on $d$ (correlation 0.78, alignment factor 1.55, SCA shares) is scoped to it.
  - $d_k$ is named the **dominance effect**, the functional departure of the heterozygote from the homozygote midpoint, a property of the locus; "dominance deviation" is reserved for Fisher's statistical object (05b:145-166, @sec-heterosis-dk-meaning). The convention is declared for 05b, 00, 01, 02, 02b and says that other chapters (05 among them) still use the older name (05b:152-155); it is true of those five chapters after this review. $a$ is a scale, never computed (05b:131-143). $G(\mathbf x)$ and $H_i = \sum_k d_k\mathbb 1\{x_{ik} = 0.5\}$ (@eq-het-genotypic-value, @eq-het-value, 05b:180-206); `heterosis_value()` takes no additive effect; oracle `ref_heterosis()` on 25 random hybrids, gap 0 (@tbl-het-oracle-gap).
  - $\mathbb E[H]$ (@eq-het-expected, 05b:239-265) and the split $p_A + p_B - 2p_Ap_B = 2\bar p(1-\bar p) + y^2/2$ (@eq-het-split, 05b:267-282). Under flat dominance ($d_k \equiv 1$), inbred parents and the complete A x B factorial the two terms are exactly $GD_T$ and $GD_{BS}$ of `gd_partition()` (asserted, $10^{-12}$ relative; 0.3281 and 0.0336, sum 0.3617 as in 00:435-447 and 01:360-380); for varying $d$, $GD_{BS}$ is the unweighted version and the identity holds for the $d$-weighted term only (05b:297-306). $GD_T = GD_{BI} + GD_{BS}$, so expected F1 heterozygosity is $GD_{BI} + 2GD_{BS}$; the random-mating textbook heterosis $\sum_k d_ky_k^2$ (Falconer & Mackay, locator UNVERIFIED) is twice the divergence term, checked (@tbl-het-falconer-ch14, 05b:308-345). $GD_T + GD_{BS} = 1 - \theta_{AB}$ (@eq-het-theta-ab, 05b:349-380; $\theta_{AB}$ identified with $f_{AB}$ of 02 and $\bar f^L_{AB}$ of 01) and $2\,GD_{WI\text{-}hyb} = GD_T + GD_{BS}$ with $GD_{WI} = 0$ (@eq-het-wi-hyb, 05b:382-418), asserted.
  - Inbreeding depression $ID = 2F\sum_k d_kp_kq_k$ (@eq-het-id, 05b:420-455): at $F = 1$ it is the shared term at $p_A = p_B$ (asserted); closed form 27.5856 against the mean of 8 simulated runs 27.5746 at $F = 0.6$ (300 loci, one pool), $z = -1.2$. An inbred line's dominance value is exactly 0 (vacuous on simulated 0/1 lines, informative on real genotypes). Therefore $ID$ is not the current-cycle price of the $\alpha$ budget, which acts on $GD_T = GD_{BI} + GD_{BS}$ (not $GD_{BI}$ alone); whether the cost arrives later in the additive variance is the conditional question of @sec-response-genic, not tested here. For $0 < F < 1$ **this book does not model depression** (04's P9; only use at $F = 1$ in @sec-alphasimr; the $F = 0.6$ evaluation is a formula check) (05b:505-542).
  - Genetic distance predicts heterosis exactly only under flat $d$, inbred parents, complete factorial: $H_{ij} = d\,m(1 - f^L_{ij}) = d\,m\,\mathrm{MRD}^2_{ij}$ (@eq-het-distance, asserted); under the gamma $d$ the correlation falls to 0.78 (05b:544-600).
  - **$F_{BN}$ against $F_{ST}$** (05b:617-628): $F_{BN}$ is the Balding-Nichols parameter (`fst` in the code), used in every expectation and equation; $F_{ST}$ only for the statistic $GD_{BS}/GD_T$ of @eq-f-stats (Caballero & Toro 2002 Eq. 8), which estimates about $F_{BN}/(2 - F_{BN})$. "A function of the frequencies only" means the statistic. Agrees with 00:561-585 and 90:150-161.
  - **Neutral divergence adds no expected heterosis** (05b:602-787): $\mathbb E[2\bar p(1-\bar p)] = 2p^{*}(1-p^{*})(1 - F_{BN}/2)$ and $\mathbb E[y^2/2] = F_{BN}p^{*}(1-p^{*})$, equal and opposite, exactly in expectation (@eq-het-bn-total, @eq-het-bn-halves); $GD_T$ pays unit for unit and $GD_{WS}$ falls by **twice** what $GD_{BS}$ gains (derived from $2\bar p\bar q = p_Aq_A + p_Bq_B + y^2/2$, not measured on the sweep). The sweep, one draw per row, seed 5, teaching scale (@tbl-het-sweep, @fig-heterosis-sweep): six totals 0.3620-0.3664 (predicted level 0.365, mean 0.3646); divergence rises 0.0107 -> 0.0846 (gain 0.0739), shared falls 0.3549 -> 0.2792 (loss 0.0756), 2 % apart; sample-frequency offset $\propto (1 - F_{BN})$ explained and compared. The sweep is consistent with the expectation, it does not measure it.
  - **What buys heterosis is divergence aligned with dominance** (05b:789-900, @eq-het-alignment): divergence term $= \tfrac m2[\bar d\,\overline{y^2} + \mathrm{cov}(d, y^2)]$; same pools, same $F_{ST}$, same set of dominance effects, three placements: 0.79 / 1.00 / 1.23 of the independent total (factor 1.55, one pool pair, one $d$); stratified within groups of equal shared weight isolates the divergence channel. `mean_sq_delta_p` is twice the divergence term (@tbl-het-msdp, 05b:901-930).
  - **GCA/SCA of heterosis** (05b:932-1130): $H_{ij} = \mu + g^A_i + g^B_j + s_{ij}$ (@eq-het-gca-sca, @eq-het-gca-sca-terms), exact and orthogonal, reconstruction and `lm()` equivalence asserted (7.96e-13 and 6.97e-12 against a tolerance of 3.9e-7). **Declaration** (05b:961-973): these symbols denote the decomposition of $H_{ij}$, not of the cross mean $\bar y_{ij}$ of @eq-hp-gca-sca; the chapter's $g^A_i$ is the **dominance part** of 00's $g^A_i$, the additive part $\sum_k a_k\delta u_{ik}$ having cancelled; the qualifier "GCA for heterosis" for this term and "GCA" unqualified for 00's; $s_{ij}$ is the same object in both, $\mu$ and $g$ are not. GCA is an average effect at the **opposite** pool's frequencies ($p_B = 0.5$ contributes zero to $g^A$), so combining ability is a property of a line against a tester pool (@sec-heterosis-gca-average, 05b:1033-1086; agrees with 00:120-132 and 02b). $s_{ij} = -d\,m\,\mathrm{DC}(f^L)_{ij}$ under flat $d$ (@eq-het-sca-dc); variance components @eq-het-var-components (closed forms for independent-Bernoulli lines).
  - Tester-based mean of $H$ (the book's own derivation, 05b:1059-1086): over all $n_B$ testers the mean is exactly $\mu + g^A_i$; over a sample of $k$ it carries $\bar g^B_T$ (level, not ranking) and $\bar s_{i\cdot T}$ (in the ranking, mean zero in expectation); a testcross mean also carries the candidate's additive part; lines only, no mapping onto any published scheme.   - **Divergence destroys SCA faster than it builds GCA** (05b:1177-1366): $\sigma^2_{SCA,k}$ in expectation $\propto (1 - F_{BN})^2$; GCA a concave parabola in $F_{BN}$ with turning point $F^\star_k = (8p^{*}_k(1-p^{*}_k) - 1)/(8p^{*}_k(1-p^{*}_k))$ (@eq-het-var-bn, @eq-het-gca-peak, @eq-het-sca-gca-ratio). Sweep (seed 5 pools, seed 4 $d$, one draw per row): SCA variance 91.8 -> 27.8, GCA 60.0 -> 77.9 (+30 %, recovers 28 % of what SCA lost), total 151.9 -> 105.7 (-30 %), SCA share 0.605 -> 0.263; grid maximum over pool seeds 1-20 "0.20 in 1, 0.30 in 10, 0.45 in 9", expected peak on the $F_{BN}$ axis 0.36. ## 03-simulating-crosses -- Simulating lines, pools and crosses
- Question: how the stage-1 objects the rest of the book consumes (`X`, `f`, `hybrids`, optional `fL`, and the `ctx` built from them) are generated, which assumptions the generator makes, what its divergence knob `fst` does to the heterozygous fraction and the gene-diversity components, and what its traits do and do not model. It follows 05b because the Foundations chapters drew their data from `simulate_pools()` (02b) and `simulate_lines()`/`simulate_data()` (05b) without deriving them, and 02b and 05b both announce 03 as the next step (03:19-47). It is a generator chapter: it derives no new population-genetic result and claims no heterosis (sec-sim-divergence, 03:361-376).
- Establishes:
  - Opening (03:19-47): `simulate_pools()` and `simulate_data()` consume R's random stream in the same order up to the lines, so at `book_cfg`'s values they return the same lines and the same hybrid matrix `X` (asserted in the chunk of @tbl-line-genotypes, @sec-sim-lines, 03:122-134), and only the traits differ (`n_qtl` loci with independent effects plus noise against correlated effects at every marker and no noise). `simulate_pools()` clamps pool frequencies to $[10^{-6}, 1 - 10^{-6}]$ and `simulate_lines()` does not, so the pool frequencies agree to within $10^{-6}$ and the lines exactly at those arguments. 02b's chunk asserts `cfg$fst == book_cfg$fst` and `cfg$seed == book_cfg$seed` (02b:63-64).
  - The stage-1 contract (@tbl-stage1-contract, 03:56-75): `X` N x m in {0, 0.5, 1}, `f` N x N, `hybrids` N x (3 + n_traits), optional `fL` L x L (coancestry between lines); stage 2 runs without `fL` and loses only the heterotic-group decomposition.
  - Generator (@sec-sim-lines, @eq-balding-nichols, 03:76-137): Balding-Nichols-type model with the name and the one-parameter idea taken from @balding1995 and the Beta densities and the reading of $F_{BN}$ declared the book's own specification, not quoted from the paper. $p^{*}_k \sim U(0.05, 0.95)$, $q^{*}_k = 1 - p^{*}_k$; $p_{Ak}, p_{Bk}$ independent Beta draws with mean $p^{*}_k$ and variance $F_{BN}\,p^{*}_k q^{*}_k$ (derived from $ab/[(a+b)^2(a+b+1)]$, $a + b = (1 - F_{BN})/F_{BN}$): $F_{BN}$ is the divergence of each pool from the ancestral frequency, $\mathrm{Var}(p_{Ak} - p_{Bk}) = 2F_{BN}p^{*}_kq^{*}_k$, and the statistic $F_{ST} = GD_{BS}/GD_T$ on two pools of unbounded size estimates about $F_{BN}/(2 - F_{BN})$ = 0.081 at 0.15. Lines are $2 \times$ Bernoulli, so every line is homozygous (0 or 2 only, @tbl-line-genotypes).
  - **$p^{*}_k$ against `ctx$p0`** (03:93-97, 03:558-567): $p^{*}_k$ is a parameter of the draw, never observed in the data, and is neither $p_{Ak}$, $p_{Bk}$ nor `ctx$p0`; `ctx$p0` is `colMeans(X)` of the hybrids, a sample quantity, equal for equal pools and the complete factorial to the mean of the two pools' sampled line frequencies (asserted, relative deviation 1.1e-16); `maf_pop = pmin(p0, 1 - p0)`; `p0` is the frozen base of $F_{hom}$ and $F_{drift}$ (@sec-genomic-era), never recomputed per scenario.
  - **The generator's declared assumptions** (@sec-sim-assumptions, 03:141-176): biallelic, neutral, unlinked markers (drift alone from one ancestral frequency vector, no selection, mutation or migration after the split; $p^{*}_k$, $p_{Ak}$, $p_{Bk}$ and every genotype independent across $k$; no map, no recombination, no linkage disequilibrium beyond sampling noise); unrelated lines drawn independently given the pool frequency and inbred by fiat at fixation (no doubled-haploid or selfing path, @sec-hp-cycle); equal pool sizes ($n_A = n_B$) and the complete A x B factorial with equal weights, an assumption and not a property of hybrid programmes; additive traits with effects at every marker, known without error. The reported numbers are conditional on these; the exact identities (per-hybrid heterozygous fraction $= 1 - f^L_{ab}$ for any inbred parents, $\mathbf f$ = `tcrossprod(cbind(X, 1 - X))/m` for any 0/0.5/1 matrix, $\sum\mathbf G = 0$ for any sample-centred panel) do not need them all; independence across loci enters the dispersion of the replicates, not the expectations. Relaxations are pointed to: linkage, outbred founders, directional dominance, several generations in @sec-alphasimr; which crosses in @sec-mating and @sec-joint; dominance in @sec-heterosis and @sec-alphasimr; prediction error in @sec-genomic-prediction.
  - **The divergence knob, $F_{BN}$ grid at $m$ = 2000** (@sec-sim-divergence, @fig-divergence-het, @tbl-divergence-grid, @tbl-divergence-trade, 03:178-335): teaching lines (25 + 25), $m$ = `book_cfg$m` = 2000, six $F_{BN}$ from 0.02 to 0.40, one draw per point; the 0.15 row is the F1 of the chapter's own `L` and the value of @tbl-diagonal: **heterozygous fraction 0.3617**, $GD_{BS}$ 0.0336, $GD_T$ 0.3281, $GD_{WS}$ 0.2945 (asserted against `L`, deviation 1.1e-16).   - The expectation (03:337-360, under the assumptions of @sec-sim-assumptions): per hybrid the heterozygous fraction is exactly $1 - f^L_{ab}$ (asserted in the chunk of @tbl-ctx-check); its mean over the complete factorial is $1 - \bar f^{L}_{AB}$, with $\bar f^{L}_{AB}$ = `between_pool_f` (written $f_{AB}$ in @sec-subdivided and $\theta_{AB}$ in @sec-notation, identified on the complete factorial in @sec-heterosis); with equal pool sizes $1 - \bar f^L_{AB} = GD_T + GD_{BS}$ exactly.
