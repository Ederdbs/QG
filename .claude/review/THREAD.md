## 01-coancestry -- Coancestry, and the two matrices
- Question: how the hybrid marker matrix $\mathbf X$ is built from the line genotypes, why the VanRaden $\mathbf G$ cannot measure diversity (the total of its elements is zero by construction), and what the molecular coancestry $\mathbf f$ used instead is, including what its scale is and is not, and the two published treatments that arrive at it. It follows 00 because 00 has already defined IBD/IBS, coancestry, $s_i$, $F_i$ and $GD_S = 1 - \theta_S$ in words and points here for @eq-x-freq, @eq-hybrid-dosage and @eq-f-molecular; 01 supplies that computation and the contrast with $\mathbf G$ (01:8-22).
- Establishes:
  - Hybrid dosage $M^H_{(ab)k} = (M^L_{ak} + M^L_{bk})/2 \in \{0,1,2\}$ from homozygous parents, a deterministic genotype (@eq-hybrid-dosage, 01:24-45); F1 heterosis = sum of $d_k$ over the loci where the parents differ, no epistasis, $d_k$ defined at first use as the departure of the heterozygote from the homozygote midpoint (01:41-46, pointing to @eq-het-value); $x_{ik} = M^H_{ik}/2 \in \{0, 0.5, 1\}$ and $\mathbf X = \mathbf M^H/2$ (@eq-x-freq, 01:48-61).
  - VanRaden $\mathbf G = \mathbf Z\mathbf Z'/(2\sum_k p_k(1-p_k))$ with $p_k$ in the hybrid population (@eq-vanraden-g, 01:71-80); `vanraden_G()` takes the 0/1/2 matrix $\mathbf M^H$ and returns a list `G, Z, p`, while `molecular_coancestry()` takes 0/0.5/1 $\mathbf X$ (01:81-90).
  - $\mathbf 1'\mathbf G\mathbf 1 = 0$ (@eq-g-sum-zero, 01:92-102), so $\theta_S = (2n^2)^{-1}\mathbf 1'\mathbf G_{SS}\mathbf 1 = 0$ and $N_s = 1/(2\theta_S)$ diverges on $\mathbf G$: `sum_G` $-4.179 \times 10^{-11}$, `Ns_on_G` $-9.349 \times 10^{15}$ (round-off reciprocal, no information), $\min \mathbf G = -0.1389$ (@tbl-g-sums, 01:103-134). $\mathbf G\mathbf 1 = \mathbf 0$, so $\mathbf G$ is singular (01:412-416).
  - On $\mathbf f$ (biallelic markers) $\theta_S \in [1/2, 1]$ and $N_s \in [1/2, 1]$ for every set: whole population 0.7442, 1000 random subsets 0.6943-0.7445 (@tbl-ns-on-f, 01:111-114, 01:137-150); a monotone rescaling, not a head count (@sec-ns).
  - Subset sum under random sampling without replacement: $\mathrm E[\mathbf 1_S'\mathbf G\mathbf 1_S] = n\frac{N-n}{N-1}\overline G_{ii}$, not a function of $n/N$ alone, zero at $n = N$; Monte Carlo 75.7129 against formula 75.5192, s.e. 0.1065 (n = 100, 20000 draws, @tbl-subset-sum-mc), and exactly $n\overline G_{ii} + n(n-1)(\sum G - \operatorname{tr}G)/(N(N-1))$ (01:152-195). A subset sum is a well-defined distance from the population centroid, not a coancestry of the subset (01:165-168).
  - The failure of $\mathbf G$ is that its centring constant $2\mathbf p$ is estimated from the set being measured, so the reference moves with the set (callout, 01:198-208).
  - Molecular coancestry $f_{ij} = m^{-1}\sum_k [x_{ik}x_{jk} + (1-x_{ik})(1-x_{jk})]$, identity in state, $f_{ij} \in [0, 1]$ (@eq-f-molecular, 01:210-234); matrix form $\mathbf f = m^{-1}(2\mathbf X\mathbf X' - \mathbf X\mathbf 1\mathbf 1' - \mathbf 1\mathbf 1'\mathbf X' + m\mathbf J)$, equal to `tcrossprod(cbind(X, 1 - X))/m`, a Gram matrix (@eq-f-matrix, 01:258-284); book data: min 0.6448, max 0.8318, diagonal mean 0.8192, off-diagonal mean 0.6717 (@tbl-f-summary).
  - $s_i = f_{ii} = (1+F_i)/2$ with $F_i$ numerically the fraction of markers at which $i$ is homozygous, an individual quantity and not $F_{hom}$ (01:229-234, 01:347-359); the minimum of the diagonal is 0.5, reached by a hybrid heterozygous at every marker (01:338-344).
  - What the scale of $\mathbf f$ is: IBS, not an unbiased IBD estimate; under the assumptions of @eq-hp-ibs-ibd, $\mathbb E[f_{ij}] = A_{ij}/2 + (1 - A_{ij}/2)\times$ base-population floor, with floor $m^{-1}\sum_k(p_{b,k}^2 + q_{b,k}^2) \ge 1/2$; $\mathbf f$ is read as a comparison between sets on the same markers, not an absolute coancestry; no constant is estimated from the set measured (01:235-256).
  - Two floors, two names: the base-population floor (base frequencies $p_{b,k}$, 01:248-250, 01:443-446) and the panel floor $m^{-1}\sum_k(p_k^2 + q_k^2)$ with $p_k$ the frequency in the $N$ hybrids, an exact identity of the set: $\operatorname{mean}(\mathbf f) =$ panel floor (asserted at $10^{-12}$), off-diagonal mean from it and the diagonal; panel floor 0.6719, off-diagonal 0.6717; same hybrids on MAF $> 0.30$ markers 0.5271, on MAF $< 0.15$ markers 0.8710 (@tbl-panel-floor, 01:305-336).
  - Heterozygous fraction: $2(1 - \bar s) = 1 - \bar F = 1 - \bar f^L_{AB} = 0.3617$ on the complete A x B factorial of inbred lines, an identity of the realised factorial through $F_{(ab)} = f^L_{ab}$; independent of $F_{BN}$ under neutral Balding-Nichols divergence because the F1 is heterozygous with expected probability $2p^{*}_k(1-p^{*}_k)$ ($p^{*}_k$ the ancestral frequency of the draw, @eq-balding-nichols in 03, not the frozen base $p_{0,k}$); nothing claimed for non-neutral divergence (@tbl-diagonal, 01:360-380). ## 02-subdivided -- Diversity in a subdivided population
- Question: how the gene diversity of a metapopulation splits into within-individual, between-individual and between-subpopulation terms (Caballero & Toro 2002), what the F statistics, the per-subpopulation metrics and the effective-size result say about it, and which chapter of this book manages each term. It follows 01 because 01 defines the molecular coancestry $\mathbf f$ between single individuals; 02 averages the same quantity over the individuals of subpopulations, states the partition in those terms, and then makes the step to hybrids partitioned over parent lines (02:5-24, @sec-keystone).
- Establishes:

## 05b-heterosis-dominance -- Heterosis, dominance, and what divergence buys
- Question: whether between-pool divergence is "the heterosis engine" that the book had asserted since 02, and what follows once the claim is derived: the expected heterosis of a pool pair, its exact split into a shared term ($GD_T$) and a divergence term ($GD_{BS}$) under stated conditions, why neutral divergence relocates expected heterosis from the one to the other without adding any, what does buy heterosis (divergence aligned with dominance), the combining-ability decomposition of heterosis (GCA/SCA) and how neutral divergence reshapes it, and eight steps to run this on a programme's data.   - Generator (@sec-sim-lines, @eq-balding-nichols, 03:76-137): Balding-Nichols-type model with the name and the one-parameter idea taken from @balding1995 and the Beta densities and the reading of $F_{BN}$ declared the book's own specification, not quoted from the paper. $p^{*}_k \sim U(0.05, 0.95)$, $q^{*}_k = 1 - p^{*}_k$; $p_{Ak}, p_{Bk}$ independent Beta draws with mean $p^{*}_k$ and variance $F_{BN}\,p^{*}_k q^{*}_k$ (derived from $ab/[(a+b)^2(a+b+1)]$, $a + b = (1 - F_{BN})/F_{BN}$): $F_{BN}$ is the divergence of each pool from the ancestral frequency, $\mathrm{Var}(p_{Ak} - p_{Bk}) = 2F_{BN}p^{*}_kq^{*}_k$, and the statistic $F_{ST} = GD_{BS}/GD_T$ on two pools of unbounded size estimates about $F_{BN}/(2 - F_{BN})$ = 0.081 at 0.15. Lines are $2 \times$ Bernoulli, so every line is homozygous (0 or 2 only, @tbl-line-genotypes).
  - **$p^{*}_k$ against `ctx$p0`** (03:93-97, 03:558-567): $p^{*}_k$ is a parameter of the draw, never observed in the data, and is neither $p_{Ak}$, $p_{Bk}$ nor `ctx$p0`; `ctx$p0` is `colMeans(X)` of the hybrids, a sample quantity, equal for equal pools and the complete factorial to the mean of the two pools' sampled line frequencies (asserted, relative deviation 1.1e-16); `maf_pop = pmin(p0, 1 - p0)`; `p0` is the frozen base of $F_{hom}$ and $F_{drift}$ (@sec-genomic-era), never recomputed per scenario.
  - **The generator's declared assumptions** (@sec-sim-assumptions, 03:141-176): biallelic, neutral, unlinked markers (drift alone from one ancestral frequency vector, no selection, mutation or migration after the split; $p^{*}_k$, $p_{Ak}$, $p_{Bk}$ and every genotype independent across $k$; no map, no recombination, no linkage disequilibrium beyond sampling noise); unrelated lines drawn independently given the pool frequency and inbred by fiat at fixation (no doubled-haploid or selfing path, @sec-hp-cycle); equal pool sizes ($n_A = n_B$) and the complete A x B factorial with equal weights, an assumption and not a property of hybrid programmes; additive traits with effects at every marker, known without error. The reported numbers are conditional on these; the exact identities (per-hybrid heterozygous fraction $= 1 - f^L_{ab}$ for any inbred parents, $\mathbf f$ = `tcrossprod(cbind(X, 1 - X))/m` for any 0/0.5/1 matrix, $\sum\mathbf G = 0$ for any sample-centred panel) do not need them all; independence across loci enters the dispersion of the replicates, not the expectations.
