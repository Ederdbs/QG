## Findings

### Disqualifying (statement contradicted by the chapter's own output, or by an approved chapter)

**12-R1** (12:118-120) "A metric near zero is not distinguishing this plan from chance, and cannot be serving as a constraint." Contradicted by the table directly above it: `z_GD` is 0.00 at `DE_alpha_0.00`, the scenario in which GD is the binding constraint (achieved alpha -8e-6). Over seeds 1-6 `z_GD` at alpha = 0 is 0.00, -0.12, -0.08, 0.13, -0.03, 0.05. It is zero by construction, because alpha is measured against the mean of the same null. The informative z-scores are those of the unconstrained lens (`z_Ne_parents` -5.6, `z_F_drift` +7.7 at alpha = 0); the sentence has to say that a constraint is read at its own budget, not at zero.

**12-R2** (12:74-89) The N_s vs N_e,par contrast. On molecular `f`, `N_s = 1/(2 theta)` is in [1/2, 1] for every set (06 @sec-ns; `R/01_metrics.R`: "not a number of individuals"); the chapter prints 0.742 to 0.728 and `Ns_relative` 0.975 to 1, then concludes "A programme reporting only N_s would see a healthy pool". (i) A ratio to the maximum of a quantity floored at 0.5 has no meaning; "healthy" is not defined on that scale. (ii) N_s is a monotone rescaling of the quantity the DE constrains (alpha is 0 to 3.9 %, so N_s moves ~1.5 % by algebra): the contrast restates that alpha pins theta, it is not an independent finding. (iii) One dataset, one DE run per budget, no dispersion. Rewrite as: theta is pinned, a head-count-scale metric (`Ne_parents`, 27.5 to 13.5; random 37.3) is not. Do not normalise N_s by its maximum. 05:1821 and 05:2145-2149 promise this table, so it stays; its reading changes.
