A.1.3 The partition

Component Definition Meaning In hybrid breeding
\(GD_T\) \(1 - \bar f\) total the pool’s diversity
\(GD_{WI}\) \(1 - \tilde s\) within individuals 0 for inbred lines; use GD_WI_hyb for the F1s
\(GD_{BI}\) \(\tilde s - \tilde f\) between individuals, within subpopulation segregating variation available to selection
\(GD_{WS}\) \(1 - \tilde f\) within subpopulations within-pool diversity; equals \(GD_{BI}\) with inbred lines, the term recurrent selection erodes (Table 3.5)
\(GD_{BS}\) \(\tilde f - \bar f\) between subpopulations the heterosis engine – maintain, do not maximise (Chapter 5)

with \(GD_T = GD_{WI} + GD_{BI} + GD_{BS}\) and \(F_{ST} = GD_{BS}/GD_T\).

That \(F_{ST}\) is a statistic computed on a set of genotypes. The Balding-Nichols parameter of the simulations, fst in the code and written \(F_{BN}\) here, is a different quantity: it is the variance of a pool’s allele frequency around the ancestral one, in units of \(p(1-p)\) (see Terms). Chapter 5, Chapter 12, Chapter 13, Chapter 21, Chapter 25 and Appendix C write that parameter as \(F_{ST}\) wherever it is a simulation setting or a Balding-Nichols expectation; read it there as \(F_{BN}\). With two pools of unbounded size the expected \(GD_{BS}\) per locus is \(F_{BN}\,p(1-p)\) and the expected \(GD_T\) is \((2 - F_{BN})\,p(1-p)\), so the statistic estimates approximately \(F_{BN}/(2 - F_{BN})\) (a ratio of expectations), not \(F_{BN}\): at \(F_{BN}\) = 0.15 that is 0.081, against 0.081 computed from simulated frequencies. On finite pools the statistic is larger again, because \(\tilde f\) includes each line’s self-coancestry: on the 25 + 25 lines that simulate_pools() draws at fst = 0.15 (seed 1), the \(F_{ST}\) of Chapter 3 is 0.102, and 0.084 once the diagonal is dropped from both \(\tilde f\) and \(\bar f\).

A.2 Conventions

Coancestry always includes the diagonal. Group coancestry in the sense of Cockerham. That is what makes \(1 - \theta\) exactly Nei’s \(H_e\) and makes the algebra close. Drop the diagonal and none of the identities hold.

Everything is computed per locus and then averaged over loci. Never average frequencies first.

Marker coding. Lines are 0/1 (or 0/2) because they are homozygous. Hybrids are 0/1/2 as dosage, rescaled to 0/0.5/1 as within-individual frequency. stage1_build() auto-detects and rescales.

f is never centered, G always is. A negative coancestry is a symptom of the wrong matrix.

Loss is always relative to a same-size random baseline. Never to the full pool.

A.3 Terms

Balding-Nichols model. A Beta distribution with mean \(p\) and variance \(F_{BN}\,p(1-p)\), used to draw pool-specific allele frequencies from an ancestral frequency \(p\). \(F_{BN}\) (fst) is the expected divergence of each pool from the ancestral population; it is not the \(F_{ST}\) statistic of Chapter 3 computed on the pools (see the partition above).

Group coancestry. The probability that two alleles sampled at random from a whole set are identical in state, including the case of sampling the same individual twice.

Heterotic group / pool. A set of lines maintained as a breeding unit, crossed to lines of a complementary group to produce hybrids. Divergence between groups is where heterosis comes from – but only the divergence that coincides with dominance. Divergence produced by drift alone generates none, because it is paid for exactly out of the within-pool term. See Chapter 5.

Mid-parent heterosis. The F1’s value minus the mean of its two parents. With inbred parents the additive contributions cancel exactly, leaving only the dominance deviations at the F1’s heterozygous loci.

General and specific combining ability (GCA, SCA). The two halves of a hybrid’s value that a two-way layout separates: what a line contributes to every cross it enters, and what depends on which two lines met. For heterosis both are exact functions of \(d\) and the pool frequencies, with GCA weighted by the opposite pool’s frequencies – so combining ability is a property of a line against a tester pool, never of the line alone. See Section 5.10.

Reciprocal recurrent selection. Selecting on performance in cross and recycling the winners within their own pool. Selection information crosses the pool boundary; germplasm does not. See Chapter 23.

Identity by state (IBS) vs by descent (IBD). IBS is what markers observe: two alleles are the same allele. IBD adds the requirement of common ancestry from a defined base. The gap between them is where Chapter 10’s whole argument lives.

Line-level collapse. The exact identity \(\theta_S = \mathbf{w}' \mathbf{f}^L \mathbf{w}\) for F1 hybrids of homozygous lines, which removes the need for the \(N \times N\) hybrid matrix. See Section 12.1.

Status number. \(N_s = 1/(2\theta)\). A head count of unrelated non-inbred individuals only on coancestry measured against a base population; on molecular \(\mathbf{f}\), \(N_s \in [1/2, 1]\) and is a monotone rescaling of \(\theta\) (Section 11.3). A static descriptor.

Truncation selection. Take the top \(n\) by index, ignore everything else. The ceiling on gain and the floor on diversity.

Warm start. Seeding an optimiser’s initial population with heuristic solutions. Not optional here: without it the differential evolution finishes below a plain greedy.
